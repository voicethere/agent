# English practice lessons

## Ordering at a café

Useful phrases:

- Could I have a flat white, please?
- Can I get that to go?
- What do you recommend?
- Do you have anything without milk?
- Could I pay by card?

Idioms:

- "A piece of cake" means something is very easy. Example: Ordering coffee in English is a piece of cake for me now.
- "My cup of tea" means something you like. Example: Quiet cafés are my cup of tea.

## Small talk at work

Useful phrases:

- How was your weekend?
- Did you have a good commute?
- Any plans for the holidays?
- It's been a busy week, hasn't it?
- Nice to finally meet you in person.

Idioms:

- "Break the ice" means to start a conversation with people who don't know each other. Example: I asked about his trip to break the ice.
- "Catch up" means to talk about what has happened since you last met. Example: Let's catch up over lunch.

## A job interview

Useful phrases:

- Thank you for having me.
- I have three years of experience in this field.
- One of my strengths is working with a team.
- Could you tell me more about the role?
- When can I expect to hear from you?

Idioms:

- "Hit the ground running" means to start a new job and do well right away. Example: I want to hit the ground running in my first month.
- "Think outside the box" means to think in a new, creative way. Example: The team wants someone who can think outside the box.

## At the doctor

Useful phrases:

- I've had a sore throat since Monday.
- It hurts when I breathe in.
- I'm allergic to penicillin.
- How often should I take this medicine?
- Do I need to come back for a check-up?

Idioms:

- "Under the weather" means a little sick. Example: I'm feeling under the weather today.
- "Back on your feet" means well again after an illness. Example: You'll be back on your feet in a few days.

## Travel and directions

Useful phrases:

- Excuse me, how do I get to the station?
- Is it within walking distance?
- Turn left at the traffic lights.
- Which platform does the train to Boston leave from?
- Could you tell me when we get to my stop?

Idioms:

- "Off the beaten track" means away from the usual tourist places. Example: We found a small village off the beaten track.
- "On the right track" means doing something the correct way. Example: Keep going, you're on the right track.

## Phone calls

Useful phrases:

- Hello, this is Maria speaking.
- Could I speak to Mr. Brown, please?
- I'm calling about my reservation.
- Could you repeat that, please?
- I'll call you back later.

Idioms:

- "Hang on" means wait a moment. Example: Hang on, I'll get a pen.
- "Get back to someone" means to reply later. Example: I'll get back to you by tomorrow.

## Making plans

Useful phrases:

- Are you free on Saturday afternoon?
- How about we meet at six?
- Shall we get dinner after the film?
- That works for me.
- Let's keep in touch about the details.

Idioms:

- "Up in the air" means not decided yet. Example: Our holiday plans are still up in the air.
- "Play it by ear" means to decide as things happen. Example: We'll play it by ear and see how the weather is.

## Giving opinions

Useful phrases:

- In my opinion, this is a good idea.
- I see what you mean, but I disagree.
- As far as I'm concerned, it's worth the price.
- I'm not sure I agree.
- That's a fair point.

Idioms:

- "Take it with a grain of salt" means do not believe it completely. Example: Take online reviews with a grain of salt.
- "On the fence" means not able to decide between two choices. Example: I'm on the fence about moving to a new city.
